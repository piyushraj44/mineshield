Engineering model

Hydraulic leg force is modelled as F = P × A, where P is leg pressure and A is the piston area. 1 bar = 105 Pa, so F [MN] = P [bar] × 105 × A / 106.

Piston area in configuration: m². Example: 300 bar × A = MN.

Setting pressure is the pressure the shield is re-set to when it takes roof load. Yield is where the leg's yield (relief) valve opens; the model caps leg pressure there.

Each shield repeats a cycle: advancing (legs lowered, ~10 s), setting (pressure builds from the setting pressure towards the roof load, 120 s) and loaded. Roof load varies along the face and over time; the hydraulic response is a first-order lag; measurements add noise and quantisation. Pressure and gradient rules are suppressed while a shield is advancing, so its normal pressure drop is not alarmed.

The Isolation Forest is trained on normal-only synthetic data (seeds 1000–1004). Its alert threshold (score 1.0) and the 5-sample persistence were chosen on separate tuning episodes (seeds 2100–2107, python -m mineshield.tuning); the test seeds (3000+) were never used for tuning.
